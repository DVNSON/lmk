How it works

Three minutes to set up. Then it runs itself.

No tokens, no passwords, no copying assignment lists by hand.

1

Add the Chrome extension

Get it on the Chrome Web Store, then open Canvas once like you normally would. LMK reads your courses, every assignment, what you've already submitted, and your scores — and opens your plan for you.

2

Answer one question

Roughly how many hours can you study on a weekday, and on a weekend? That's the whole setup. No availability grid, no daily planning ritual.

3

Open it in the morning

You get today's list and a total: "about two hours of your two and a half, you're fine." Everything else stays out of your way until it matters.

Why it's different

It learns how long your work actually takes.

Every other planner makes you type an estimate for every task. LMK measures instead, then checks itself against reality.

⏱️

It times your work without asking

Focus sprints, the time Canvas already recorded on your quizzes, and quiet timing while you work on an assignment page. You never fill in a form.

📈

Guesses turn into knowledge

An estimate starts with a question mark and earns a check once LMK has watched you do a few. Your reading pace and your problem-set pace are learned separately.

🤝

Your class makes it smarter

Anonymous minutes per assignment are shared between students in the same course — no names, no grades. The tenth person in a class starts out already accurate.

Why I built it

I kept starting things the night before.

Canvas showed me twelve assignments and no idea which one mattered tonight. So I built the thing I wanted: something that reads my classes, understands what each assignment actually involves, and tells me the one thing to start now. It got good enough that I'm sharing it.
