LMK — Privacy Policy

Effective September 10, 2026 · Applies to the LMK app and the "LMK Today" browser extension

LMK Today is a browser extension that reads your own Canvas or D2L Brightspace assignments and feeds them into LMK, your personal assignment planner. This page explains exactly what it reads, where that data goes, and what it never does.

Age

LMK is for students 13 and over and is not directed at children under 13. LMK does not knowingly collect anything from a child under 13; if you believe a child under 13 has used it, write to hello@lmktoday.app and whatever is held under that random id will be deleted. Anyone under 18 needs a parent or guardian to agree to the terms and to make any purchase.

Sync from anywhere

So your phone stays current while your laptop is closed, LMK can read your Canvas for you from its own server. This exists for Canvas schools only: Brightspace offers students no access tokens, so LMK's server can never read a Brightspace account and the extension says so under the gear. It is offered as a ticked box on the welcome screen — on unless you untick it, and off any time under the gear — and it works like this:

  • The extension creates an access token in your own Canvas account (the same thing you could make under Account → Settings → New Access Token). Canvas lists it under Approved Integrations as "LMK"; you can delete it there at any time, and it expires on its own after six months.
  • Our server stores that token encrypted (with a key that lives only in the server's secret store, never in the database), plus the same assignment rows the extension collects: titles, due dates, submission state, scores. It stores no name, no email, no Canvas user id. Each student is a random sync id.
  • Every couple of minutes the server reads a few courses for the student whose data is oldest, and it reads yours when you open LMK and its copy is stale.
  • Turn off under the gear deletes the token and every row from our server and, on your next Canvas visit, removes the token from Canvas too.

Students on a phone with no laptop can paste a token they made themselves; the rest is identical.

The tutor

The tutor inside LMK sends your question, the conversation so far, and this one assignment's brief to LMK's server, which passes them to Anthropic's Claude and streams the answer back. The brief is what the Study screen shows you: the title, course, due date, points and submission type, the instructions and rubric from Canvas, any notes you typed for it, LMK's time estimate, your study budget for today, the titles and due dates of up to five other things due in the coming week, your current standing in that one course as a letter and a percentage, the most recent comment your instructor left on your work in that course, and the mode you picked (explain, outline, check my work). On the paid plan that includes it, you can attach a PDF or text file and photograph a problem: the file is read on your own device and only the one you pick is sent, a photo is shrunk on your device before it travels, and neither is stored on LMK's server after the answer comes back. A quiz or a deck of flashcards is one message: the same brief goes up once and the whole quiz or deck comes back; your answers stay on your device and are summarised in the conversation so the tutor can follow up. Cards you make are kept on your device and in your own Drive, and come back for review on a schedule; reviewing them sends nothing anywhere. Anthropic does not use API traffic to train its models. LMK sends no name, no email, no individual scores, and nothing from your other courses; the request is tied to the same random sync id as everything else. Use is metered in units so the feature can exist at all: a free account gets a one-off trial that does not refill, a paid plan gets units each day resetting at midnight UTC. The count lives on the server next to that random id. The buttons that open Claude, ChatGPT or Gemini in their own apps send nothing through LMK at all.

What the extension reads

  • Your Canvas or Brightspace course and assignment data: course names, assignment titles, due dates, points, submission state, scores, your course grade as Canvas calculates it, comments your instructors left on your submissions, and the time Canvas recorded on your quiz attempts. It reads this through Canvas's own API using the login session already in your browser. It never sees or stores your password.
  • Time you spend working: while a Canvas or Brightspace assignment page, its quiz, or the external course tool you launched from it (for example MyEducator or Edfinity) is visible and you are active, the extension counts minutes toward that assignment. It records only a minute tally per assignment. It does not record what you type, read, or click.

Where the data goes

  • Everything the extension collects is stored in your own browser (extension storage) and handed to the LMK page when you open it.
  • By default, everything stays on the device you are using. Nothing is uploaded.
  • If you choose to sign in with Google, LMK saves your data as a file named LMK-data.json in your own Google Drive so your phone and laptop stay in sync. LMK uses Google's drive.file permission, which lets it see only the one file it created — it can never read anything else in your Drive. Delete that file, or revoke LMK's access in your Google account, and the synced copy is gone.
  • If you turn on Keep your phone fresh, the extension itself saves each Canvas sync as a second file, LMK-canvas.json, in your own Google Drive, under the same drive.file permission, so your phone can pick it up without your laptop having LMK open. This is off until you turn it on, and the sign-in token stays inside the extension on your computer. It is never sent to us.
  • Inside claude.ai only: the copy of LMK that runs as a Claude artifact shares anonymous course statistics with other users of that copy: an assignment's Canvas ID, a median number of minutes, and a type vote (for example "done in class"). This never includes your name, your scores, your grades, or which assignments you completed. The app at lmktoday.app shares nothing of the kind — this sharing needs Claude's own storage, which a normal web page cannot reach, so on the website your timings never leave your device.

What it never does

  • It never sends your data to the extension's developers or to any advertising or analytics service.
  • It never sells, rents, or shares personal information with anyone.
  • It never changes anything in Canvas or Brightspace. It only reads.
  • It never runs on sites other than Canvas, Brightspace, the course tools listed in its permissions, and the LMK page itself.

Permissions, and why

  • storage: to keep the collected assignment data and minute tallies in your browser between visits.
  • Canvas and Brightspace hosts (canvas.asu.edu, instructure.com, brightspace.com, and any address you approve yourself in the extension): to read your assignments through the site's own API and to time work on assignment pages.
  • Course tool hosts (MyEducator, Edfinity, McGraw-Hill Connect, Macmillan, Pearson, Cengage, zyBooks, WebAssign): only in the full build LMK's author runs, to time work you do there after launching from a Canvas assignment. The build in the Chrome Web Store does not ask for these and does not time course tools.
  • lmktoday.app: to deliver the collected data to the LMK page. The full build also asks for claude.ai, where a private copy of the app runs; the Chrome Web Store build does not.

What LMK counts

To know whether anyone uses it, the LMK web app sends five plain numbers to a server run by LMK: one open per device per day, one install the first time a device opens the app, one first sync the first time Canvas data arrives, one synced per day from a device that holds Canvas data, and one extension the first time the browser extension says hello. That is the whole message: an event name and the date, in UTC. No identifier, no cookie, no IP address is stored, so a count can never be traced back to a person. Inside claude.ai this counter is off entirely. The app also fetches one small settings file from the same server when it opens, holding an announcement and the store link; that request carries nothing about you.

Two more things reach that server. When you finish the welcome screen the app makes a random id for this device and the server keeps it with a first-seen date, so a plan you buy can follow you to another device; it carries no name, no email and no coursework. And when you use the tutor, a hashed form of your network address is kept for a day so the free allowance is shared fairly across a campus; the hash cannot be turned back into the address.

The extension's morning notification is built on your own computer from the plan the LMK page already showed you. Nothing about it is sent anywhere.

A school's Brightspace, once. The first time the extension reads a Brightspace school it has not read before, it sends LMK's server that school's web address and which of Brightspace's API routes answered, as status codes and field names, plus how many courses and assignments came back. That is how LMK gets fixed for a school nobody who builds it can log in to. It carries no name, no id, no title, no grade and nothing that identifies you; the address identifies the school. It happens once per school per install and can be read on the admin side only.

The morning note on a phone. If you turn that on, LMK's server holds one more thing about you, and it is an identifier: the address your browser's push service — Apple's, Google's or Mozilla's — hands out for that one browser on that one device. It is a long random web address. Anyone holding it together with LMK's private key could make that device show a notification. It is stored against the same random sync code as your Canvas data, and against nothing else: no name, no email, no account.

Beside it LMK stores up to four numbers: for each of the next four mornings worth waking you for, the moment to send the note, how many things are due, and how many minutes they add up to. Your device works those moments out on its own clock, so LMK is never told your time zone or what time you get up — only four instants in the future. They run out after four mornings, so a phone whose owner has stopped opening LMK goes quiet rather than being read a stale plan.

What the note says is put together on your phone. No assignment title, no course name and no grade is ever sent to LMK's server or through the push service. The headline — "Today: 3 things, ~2h 10m" — travels encrypted; the line naming the first thing is added by your own browser from the plan it already has. Three things delete all of it: turning the note off in LMK, blocking notifications for LMK in your browser or phone settings, or turning off Sync from anywhere. LMK also deletes the address the moment the push service says the browser is gone.

Deleting your data

Removing the extension deletes everything it stored in your browser. Deleting the file named LMK-data.json from your Google Drive removes your synced LMK data. Turning off the morning note, or turning off Sync from anywhere, deletes the push address and the four instants stored with it. Anonymous course statistics, which exist only inside the claude.ai copy, cannot be traced back to you and are not deleted individually.

Changes and contact

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes with it. Questions: hello@lmktoday.app.
